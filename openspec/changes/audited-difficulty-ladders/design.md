# Design

## Why one axis at a time

The first audit raised all five axes together and every family read as a wall. The axes are
declared independent and the curriculum advances them independently, so a compound sweep
compounds the demand and measures a scenario no policy is ever given. Sweeping one axis with
the rest held easy isolates each axis, which is the only form in which the result is
actionable: it names the axis, not the family.

## Why two probes

Neither probe alone is sufficient, and the reason is measurable. The scripted controller
solves approach at every level and cannot score or pass at any, so its zero rows carry no
information about a ladder's shape. A trained checkpoint reaches the capability the
curriculum faces, so a ramp must exist there. The scripted controller remains necessary for
validity and for detecting an inverted axis, where a fixed controller doing better at higher
difficulty is unambiguous evidence the mapping runs backwards.

## The declared axis map

Nine gradients survive measurement across seven families. `target_width` is declared only for
`pass_receive`, where the reception window is genuinely its target, and `opponent_pressure` is
declared nowhere because no family responded to it under either probe. Declaring the map is a
reduction in claimed capability that reflects what was ever measurable, and it stops the
learning-progress rule from advancing a dimension that changes nothing.

## Compatibility

Scenario geometry changes for six families, so every parameter and state digest changes and the
generator revision is bumped. `SkillScenarioParameters` rejects a foreign revision at
construction, which turns a silent mix of holdouts into an error. No predicate, phase gate,
coefficient, or checkpoint format changes.

## Validation

Both probe matrices are recorded in `docs/evidence`. Under the capable probe every declared
axis reads as a ramp except two, which are live and not inverted. Under the scripted probe
nothing is invalid or inverted. A freshly bootstrapped policy scores 0.50 on `pass_receive` and
0.65 on `rotation_recovery`, which both scored zero at initialization and after fourteen
hundred iterations of two runs, so the phases that teach play now have a foothold.
